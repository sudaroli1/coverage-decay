# Use of AI tools

This file is the detailed record referred to by the declaration on generative AI in the
manuscript and by the *Use of AI tools* section of `README.md`. It states what was used,
when, for what, and — more usefully — what it was not used for.

## Tool

| | |
|---|---|
| Provider | Anthropic |
| Tool | Claude, accessed through the Claude desktop application |
| Model | Claude Opus 5 (`claude-opus-5`) |
| Dates of use | 20–26 August 2026 |

The commit history of this repository is the authoritative record of when the code and
the manuscript were written; `git log` gives it to the day.

## What it was used for

**1. Prose of the manuscript.** Drafting and editing, working from the author's own
results, section outlines and notes. Every section was reviewed and edited by the author
after drafting, and several were rewritten wholesale.

**2. Analysis and figure code.** Writing and reviewing the code in `src/` and
`notebooks/analysis.ipynb`. All of it is released here, so the code can be read rather
than trusted. Where a suggested implementation was wrong it was corrected against the
data; three of the nine corrections recorded in `results/2026-08-20.md` arose this way.

**3. Fifteen items in the detector validation exercise.** Of the 120 revision steps in
`results/validation/`, fifteen were discussed with the assistant during labelling:

    V001  V002  V003  V004  V005  V006  V020  V021
    V022  V023  V024  V025  V026  V044  V045

These are flagged `[supervised]` in the `notes` column of
`results/validation/sample_labels.csv`. Any reader may drop them and recompute precision
and recall on the remaining 105. They are named here, and in the manuscript, so that the
choice is available without having to take anyone's word for the count.

## What it was not used for

No measurement reported in the paper was produced, selected or interpreted by a model.

Specifically, the assistant did not choose the research question, did not define
suppression, did not decide which revisions counted, did not select which results to
report, and did not write the interpretation in Sections 5 to 7. The conception of the
study, the design of the method, every methodological decision and correction recorded in
Sections 4 and 5, and the reading of what the numbers mean are the author's.

Nor was it a source of fact. Nothing in the paper rests on a model's recollection: every
Sigma rule, commit hash, date and count cited in the text was checked against the SigmaHQ
repository or the released data before it was written down. Two examples given in early
drafts of Section 2 did not survive that check and were replaced with verified commits
(`8f22165f` and `356ab98a`).

## Verification

The author reviewed and edited all content, verified every reported figure against the
released data, and takes full responsibility for the content of the article. The figures
in the paper are generated by the scripts in `src/`; running them reproduces the numbers
in the text.

## Data submitted to the tool

The rule corpus is public (SigmaHQ, MIT-licensed) and the upstream pipeline and prepared
data are publicly released by Long and Evans (arXiv:2605.05383). No confidential,
personal or third-party proprietary data was submitted. Unpublished manuscript text and
unpublished analysis output were submitted; under Anthropic's consumer terms, whether
chat content may be used for model training is an account-level setting controlled by the
user.

## Standards this follows

Elsevier's policy on generative AI in scientific writing, which requires disclosure of
AI assistance in the writing process, prohibits listing AI as an author, and holds the
authors responsible for the work. The declaration appears in the manuscript itself, before
the reference list, as that policy requires; this file is the supporting detail.
